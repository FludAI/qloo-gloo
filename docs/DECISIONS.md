# Decision log

Choices made while building from the Gloo handoff spec (Oct 18, 2026). Product
rules from the spec stand unless a line here says otherwise.

| # | Decision | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **No spelling confirmation.** Captured words are resolved in the background and the lesson opens directly. A small "Wrong word?" link fixes rare misreads. *Overrides spec §4 "Ask the learner to confirm recognized spelling."* | Learners often can't spell or pronounce the word yet; asking them to confirm it is backwards and slows every capture. (Product owner, Oct 18.) |
| 2 | **Libraries first, LLM as backup** for word resolution: exact → spelling (Levenshtein) → sound-alike (Double Metaphone) → Hunspell dictionary. Claude is called only when the top two candidates are close or nothing scores well. | Deterministic, free, fast, testable. Thresholds: clear ≥ 0.86 with a 0.08 gap; floor 0.60 (`server/src/resolve/index.mjs`). Every resolution is logged with its method. |
| 3 | **Spoken capture is the first attempt.** When a learner says the word, the transcript is saved as their first attempt before anything is shown. | Spec requires a first attempt before reference audio. A transcript is not a pronunciation score; pronunciation stays "not assessed." |
| 4 | **OCR runs on the device** (tesseract.js). Only the recognized words reach the server, which picks the word worth learning. | No photos leave the phone; spec §13 minimal capture. |
| 5 | **New words get a provisional lesson** drafted by the agent, owned by the learner's session, labeled "Provisional draft" everywhere, with agent-suggested sources marked unverified. Attempts on drafts are stored with `review_status = provisional`. | Spec §4: unknown terms get an explicitly provisional draft, never presented as reviewed. |
| 6 | **Domain word list** ships in `server/src/resolve/domain-terms.mjs`. | General dictionaries miss terms like cholecystokinin. A fuller list (e.g. NLM MeSH) can be loaded at build time. |
| 7 | **Stack:** React + Vite + Tailwind 3.4, Express, `node:sqlite`, JavaScript (not TypeScript). | Product owner's stack preference; spec allows reusing the existing stack. No native build steps (Windows-friendly). |
| 8 | **Schedule interpretation:** review gaps are minimums from the latest qualifying attempt, not offsets from introduction; errors retry after 30 min at the same stage. | Spec §8 proposed defaults, recorded as policy `pilot-1`. |
| 9 | **Qloo through the event harness** (`qloo mcp`), not direct REST. | The hackathon supports the harness; same endpoints underneath. |
| 10 | **Hosted demo runs only this repo's code.** | Hackathon rule: the public repo must contain everything the demo needs. |
| 11 | **Camera first.** The app opens on the rear camera with a reticle; the lesson rises as a sheet over it. Tap "Gloo it" to read the framed word, or hold the mic and say the word (or "gloo it"). Permissions are requested in the tap that needs them, with photo and typing fallbacks when refused. | Phone testing on Oct 18: the page layout buried capture under text, the camera photo path failed, and clicking an existing word asked the learner to type it. (Product owner, Oct 18.) |
| 12 | **The server reads the framed crop** (Claude vision) instead of on-device OCR. Only the reticle region plus a margin leaves the phone, as one JPEG; nothing is stored. *Overrides decision 4's on-device OCR; keeps its minimal-capture intent.* | tesseract.js on a full-resolution phone photo was slow, memory-hungry and inaccurate on one word in a scene. |
| 13 | **Spoken capture goes to Google Cloud Speech-to-Text** (short model, lesson vocabulary as recognition hints) through the service account; the browser's own speech API is only a fallback. Hold-to-talk records raw 16 kHz PCM via Web Audio, so iOS and Android behave the same. | Browser speech recognition is missing on iOS Chrome and reports "not-allowed" on Safari without dictation enabled; it also cannot be told the vocabulary. |
| 14 | **Passcode gate** (`DEMO_PASSCODE`): camera, voice, the agent and lesson drafting need a passcode once per session on the hosted demo; the two reviewed lessons stay open. | Those paths spend model and speech quota; the passcode is shared with judges in the submission. |
| 15 | **Camera and mic are the interface.** The landing is a gate: turn on the phone camera and microphone, or wear smart glasses, to continue. There is no typing anywhere; the only text field is the passcode, and a shared link (`?code=`) removes even that. | Phone test on Oct 19: everything after capture still felt like a web form. (Product owner.) |
| 16 | **Favorite picks replace cue confirmation.** Each role offers 3-4 well-known names that all carry the same sound or meaning ("Which cola is yours?"); the pick is the cue, no "does this fit?" step. Picks are stored as learner cues (source `pick`) with the entity type, as taste signals for Qloo later. Provisional lessons draft their own picks. | Any option works by construction, the brand gets a view, and the pick is data. Qloo calls stay on hold until capture is solid. (Product owner.) |
| 17 | **Picture → sound → meaning, in that order, with the app talking.** Each pick card is a square stage of four pictures and one spoken line ("Say your favorite cola"). The learner says a favorite, listed or not; confetti; then the reveal shows their picture beside the word part, its approximate sound, and only then the meaning. Prompts and reveals are spoken through the browser's speech synthesis; the screen carries almost no text. The square stage scales to phone or desktop and matches the glasses display. | Owner's conjecture (Oct 22): an ambient image + sound bond to a brand the learner already likes anchors the meaning better than text. Pictures are the lead images of the matching Wikipedia articles, fetched once and cached on the server (`art.mjs`). |
| 18 | **Pieces before meaning.** The lesson no longer opens with the definition. After the first attempt the learner goes straight to one picture card per sound piece; the whole word and its meaning come after, rebuilt from the pictures they picked ("all together"). The first attempt is scored piece by piece by sound (`resolve/pieces.mjs`) and stored with the attempt as a background pronunciation signal; nothing is shown yet. | Owner (Oct 22): make sure they can name the pieces and get those stuck first. Sights and sounds connect more easily than a long string of letters; these words can't be sounded out. Gloo is a confusing-word tool, not a language tool. |
| 19 | **Motion lifted from the design tool's studies** (`web/public/motion/`, spec in `docs/MOTION.md`): the scan sweeps the frame and drops a tick per piece found; picks lock in with a ring flash and the piece rises out of the picture before its sound and then its meaning; the whole word slides together on a rail, each piece lighting as it is spoken, and glows once as a unit; practice closes a ring on a hit, shakes on a miss; progress is a bar per skill with a tick per review stage. A five-sound kit is synthesized in the browser. Defaults the studies settled: the stickiness number is never shown (ticks and color only); a word that sticks lifts off and the loop closes; effects follow the sound setting. Pieces are spoken by a respelling (`say`, e.g. koh-lee), since the engine misreads "chole". | Owner (Oct 22): make it feel like a game that got this right, not a form. The engine's mispronunciation of a piece is itself a hint the word is slippery. |
| 20 | **One server voice, paid for once.** Every spoken line comes from Google Cloud Text-to-Speech with one fixed Neural2 voice; each piece and the whole word carry IPA (`role.ipa`, `pronunciation.ipa`) that the server wraps in SSML `<phoneme>` tags, so "chole" is said exactly on every phone. Clips and pick pictures live in a Cloud Storage bucket (`media.mjs`), keyed by a hash of voice + SSML, so a sentence is synthesized once ever and survives redeploys; the owner can mark a clip "sounds right", after which it is never regenerated even if the voice or builder changes. The browser voice with the `say` respelling remains the fallback when the server has no credentials or a request fails. Chirp 3 HD was ruled out because it does not accept SSML. *Refines decision 19's respelling, which is now the fallback only.* | Browser voices read raw spellings phonetically, get them wrong, and differ by phone (Oct 24). The SQLite file on the single instance is empty after every redeploy, so anything paid for has to live in durable storage. Owner's rules: pay once per word, then never again; a clip marked correct is never resynthesized. |
| 21 | **Lessons are data, versioned, owner-edited; the AI drafts, a person decides.** Lesson packets live in the media bucket (`lessons/<id>.json`, SQLite as the hot cache), seeded once from `content/*.mjs` and never overwritten by code after that. The owner reads, patches (JSON merge patch, one version per save, last 20 versions kept), gets per-field AI suggestions for, publishes and promotes lessons through `/api/admin/lessons` behind `ADMIN_TOKEN`, or through the `/edit` console, the one place where typing is fine. A suggestion is only ever a proposal; nothing is saved until the owner accepts it and saves. The learner app re-reads the store after every save, so edits are live without a deploy. Reviewed rubrics keep their regexes as `/…/` entries in the same accept lists the drafter uses. A truthfulness rule in the draft and suggest prompts: "the word already contains its meaning" only when real morphemes were found; otherwise the summary leads with the sound hook. | Owner (Oct 25): the fixed opening line "The word already contains its meaning" is sweeping and not always true; content needs an edit path with a human/AI layer rather than a code change per wording fix. SQLite on the instance is wiped on redeploy, so anything editable has to live in the bucket. |
| 22 | **The reviewer proposes whole-lesson patches; a person accepts them one by one.** `reviewer.mjs` checks a lesson deterministically first (every answer against its own rubric through `assess()`, every pick option against the piece's sound with the first-attempt scorer, say against IPA, the meaning claim against listed components, spoken length) and then asks the model once to supply replacements and add its own, under the product rules. Model output is verified before it is shown (patchable path, real change, validating lesson, rubric-passing answer). Proposals are stored on the lesson as `pending` (no version) until `accept` applies the chosen paths as one version by `reviewer via <who>` or `discard` clears them, from the `/edit` console or the API. An MCP server (`scripts/gloo-lessons-mcp.mjs`, registered in `.mcp.json`) exposes the owner API as tools so lessons can be edited from a Claude session, with the admin token read only from the shell environment. | Owner (Oct 25): one-field suggestions don't catch a lesson that breaks a rule across fields (an option that doesn't carry the sound, an answer its rubric rejects); the owner wants to accept some of a review and drop the rest, and to run edits from Claude Code without pasting a token anywhere. The deterministic pass runs first so a dead model still reports what a machine can check. |
| 23 | **Open contribution later, on the proposal path.** Outside people and agents will edit and test lessons through the same proposal routes the owner's reviewer uses, with their own identity instead of the owner token; only accepted proposals become versions. Reward follows learner outcomes (recall, pronunciation, completed practice) rather than acceptance alone, paid as Gloo credits first and a token only if the network earns it; brand placements are disclosed and never earn contributor credit. Not before the submission. | Owner (Oct 25): make the agent and MCP available to other platforms to edit and test, for credits or crypto, Reddit-style. The Brier rule applies to contributors: score the claim against what happened. |
| 24 | **The editor is a camera too (ambient editor v2).** The console opens on the whole lesson as a map, then flies to the next field worth changing, with one proposal in focus. Say a word to replace it, keep, next, back, undo, map, by voice, swipe, keys or taps; typing stays as the owner's fallback. Proposals come from the reviewer's pending list and the per-field suggest route; keeps and skips update an editor taste profile that later becomes Qloo signals. Reference study in `web/public/design/ambient/`. | Design tool study delivered 2026-10-25 from the owner's brief: one thing in focus, the lesson around it. |
| 25 | **One keep is one version, and the rest stay pending.** The ambient console applies a reviewer proposal through `accept` with `keep_rest: true`, so a single keep lands as one version by `reviewer via owner` while the other proposals wait on the lesson; the list console's accept-selected-and-clear-the-rest is unchanged. The owner token also passes the demo gate, so the console's hold-to-talk uses server speech without a passcode. The queue is fixes, then improvements, then what the console can flag by itself; fields the grid does not show stay in the list. The taste profile lives in the owner's browser only, no Qloo call. | The study (decision 24) assumed one proposal at a time; the API's accept cleared every other proposal, which would have thrown away the rest of a review on the first keep (Oct 25). |
| 26 | **The paw's toes are syllables.** A toe lights for each syllable as the voice says the word, in time with per-syllable timestamps from the speech service; a word with more than five syllables continues on a second paw. When the learner says the word, toes light dimly live from the energy of their speech, then turn full gold for the pieces scored right. The brand guide's own rule (five toes = five stickiness ticks) stays for the meter. Animated marks in `web/public/brand/animated/`. | Owner (Oct 26): light the fingertips on each syllable as the machine says it, and as the human says it correctly. |
| 27 | **The gate is a living demo.** Before the camera opens, the landing plays the lesson loop on its own behind the copy: a word on paper, the frame, the sweep, a tick per piece, the pieces drifting apart, their hooks rising, then the meaning, then again. Nobody reads what Gloo does; they watch it. The loop is Web Animations in `AmbientGate.jsx`, lifted from the design tool's ambient landing study, stops the moment the camera is live, and collapses to one still frame under reduced motion. | 2026-10-26 |
| 28 | **Syllables are the IPA's, and the paw judges only the word.** A piece's toe count comes from its IPA (stored `ipa_syllables` or the deterministic split), checked against a hyphenated respelling; a one-word respelling such as `cola` is a hook, not a count, so chole stays two syllables said as its hook. The whole word's toes are segmented by piece only when every piece has IPA and the counts add up to the word's; otherwise the word lights as one run. In practice, the paw follows the learner's voice while they speak but settles to right or wrong only for an answer that is the word itself; any other answer leaves it idle. The voice says each syllable as its own `<phoneme>` behind a `<mark>` so the service can time it; a clip without marks spreads the syllables evenly over the word's span. | Decision 26 asked for a toe per syllable; the respellings count syllables only where they are hyphenated, and the pieces' respellings are hooks (Oct 26). Practice answers are mostly meanings, which have no syllables to light. |
| 29 | **The recognizer is told what the prompt expects.** A spoken practice answer is a word or two, and a recognizer with no context hears "bile" as "Bayou". So a practice answer names its prompt, the server hands the speech service the prompt's reviewed answer, choices, the term and its pieces as a stronger second context, and among the recognizer's readings the first the prompt accepts is the one heard. The learner never sees the hints; the answers stay server-side. | Owner's phone test (Oct 26): "bile" came back as "Bayou" and was marked wrong. |
| 30 | **Kin is said KY, and its pick is a pronunciation question.** The word says KY-nin, so the piece does too (say `ky`, IPA kaɪ), where it used to be pinned to the short i of "kin" and contradicted the whole word. Its hook is KY-netic Sand: the pick shows Kinetic Sand twice, spelled KY-netic and kih-NET-ic, and asks which the learner says; either answer teaches that here it is KY, since both pronunciations of kinetic are correct English. A pick option may carry IPA so the voice says a phonetic spelling instead of reading its letters, and a patch may remove an option. | Owner (Oct 26): the kinetic hook only sticks for people who say ky-NET-ik; ask them which they say and show both spellings. |
| 31 | **Picks are taste signals, and Qloo ranks the next pick card.** A favorite pick stores the packet's entity for that option (never the browser's word) and its Qloo ID, resolved once with `describe` and kept in the media store (`qloo/entities.json`), so a redeploy never pays for it again; a `rank` answer teaches IDs for free. From two picks on, each pick card's options are ranked by `rank` (one call per piece and entity type, two types at most) with the learner's distinct pick entities as the signals, newest first, ten at most. The editorial options are kept and only their order changes, so every option still carries the piece's sound (the reviewer's bar; an option under it is never promoted), has a picture and a connection line. A card that holds the learner's own earlier pick leads with it and that option is not sent (Qloo leaves a signal out of its own ranking). A name Qloo cannot match exactly comes back as `needs_input` with candidates: the candidate with that exact name is taken as the ID, a name with no match is dropped and remembered for a month, and the rest are ranked in one more call. No call under two picks, on a cache hit, when a piece's options are one entity, or when fewer than 30 calls remain in the day's budget; any failure leaves the editorial order. Each cue records whether its card was ranked for the learner (`hook_source`: taste, editorial, own) and where the pick sat (`shown_rank`); the cards as offered are kept per learner (`pick_offers`); progress says "3 of your 4 hooks came from your taste." Qloo stays read-only; picks never leave Gloo (`taste.mjs`). | Owner (Oct 27): build the Qloo loop for the hackathon, prioritized on long-term learner benefit: hooks that fit the person stick longer. `rank` scores our own options, where `recommend` returns entities that almost never carry the piece's sound; swapping recommended entities in waits until a hit rate is measured on real answers. Credits are a fixed scarce amount, so every call is cached and capped. |
| 32 | **The gate branches by device, and a word can arrive as a picture.** A phone looks at paper through its rear camera. A desktop's webcam faces the wrong way and the word is usually on its own screen, so the desktop gate shares a window instead (`getDisplayMedia`), with the webcam one tap away. On both, a picture of the word is a capture too: the camera roll or a photo on a phone (`capture="environment"`), the file picker, a pasted screenshot or a dropped image on a desktop. A window or picture is shown whole and dragged and zoomed under the same reticle; the server reads the framed crop as before. The mic is asked for in the same tap. | Owner (Oct 27): most laptops have a camera facing the wrong way, and sometimes a picture is easier. |
| 33 | **Gloo credits, phase 1: two words on us, one Gloo per drafted word, three per activated referral, nothing else yet.** An append-only ledger in SQLite (`server/src/credits.mjs`: `credit_events` with event type, sid, nullable account id, word experience, referral, delta, reason code and rule version, idempotency key; the balance is a sum, never a stored number). A new session gets its welcome on first contact, once for ever (the key survives "delete my data"). The one thing that costs Gloo is a word the agent has to draft: charged before the draft, refunded if it fails, never charged twice for the same word; reviewed lessons, a learner's own earlier drafts, repeat scans, reviews, practice and pronunciation retries cost nothing. At zero the glue route answers 402 (`GLOO_EMPTY`, "Ready for another word? Invite a friend to earn more Gloo.") and everything already learned stays open. Each learner has a referral code keyed from their sid (`?ref=`); a visitor's claim is recorded on arrival and pays the referrer only when the friend completes a first real word experience (a first attempt saved or skipped and a favorite picked on the same lesson), once per friend. Self-referral, a visitor with history, and duplicate claims are rejected; a claim from the referrer's own address within the hour is a soft signal: the claim stands and the reward is held for the owner to release or reverse with a logged reason (`/api/admin/credits/:sid`, `adjust`). A per-referrer cap and a promotional budget hold rewards past them; every number is an environment setting (`CREDITS_*`). The mastery bonus waits for evidence (phase 2 of the brief); referrals are the predictable path. Identity is the session cookie; sign-in later binds an account id beside the sid, so the history is never rewritten. Nothing about credits reaches Qloo; the ledger holds sids, ids and a salted address hash only. The client shows the token mark with a number, one policy line on the progress card and an Invite action (share sheet on a phone, copy on a desktop). | Owner's brief (Oct 27): keep the visible economy simple, make referrals the predictable earning path, and never make mastery a farmable rule. Decision 23 already set credits before any token. The ledger lives in the instance's SQLite like the rest of learner data, so a redeploy resets balances until the store moves; stated in the submission. |
| 34 | **The camera at the gate, the microphone at the first hold.** The gate asks for the camera (or a shared window) alone; the microphone is requested the first time the learner holds the mic, under a line that says why. A refusal of one never poisons the other, each has its own prompt with a visible reason, and each is recorded on its own in the testers report. A blocked microphone is said on the live view with the steps for that device (aA, Website Settings on iPhone) and a reload. | Human test, Oct 28: Safari's remembered Don't Allow on the microphone made the pair request fall back to camera only, and the refusal surfaced only at the first mic press as a stale message about typing. |
