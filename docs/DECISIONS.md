# Decision log

Choices made while building from the Gloo handoff spec (Oct 18, 2026). Product
rules from the spec stand unless a line here says otherwise.

| # | Decision | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **No spelling confirmation.** Captured words are resolved in the background and the lesson opens directly. A small "Wrong word?" link fixes rare misreads. *Overrides spec §4 "Ask the learner to confirm recognized spelling."* | Learners often can't spell or pronounce the word yet; asking them to confirm it is backwards and slows every capture. (Product owner, Oct 18.) |
| 2 | **Libraries first, LLM as backup** for word resolution: exact → spelling (Levenshtein) → sound-alike (Double Metaphone) → Hunspell dictionary. Claude is called only when the top two candidates are close or nothing scores well. | Deterministic, free, fast, testable. Thresholds: clear ≥ 0.86 with a 0.08 gap; floor 0.60 (`server/src/resolve/index.mjs`). Every resolution is logged with its method. |
| 3 | **Spoken capture is the first attempt.** When a learner says the word, the transcript is saved as their first attempt before anything is shown. | Spec requires a first attempt before reference audio. A transcript is not a pronunciation score; pronunciation stays "not assessed." |
| 4 | **OCR runs on the device** (tesseract.js). Only the recognized words reach the server, which picks the word worth learning. | No photos leave the phone; spec §13 minimal capture. |
| 5 | **New words get a provisional lesson** drafted by the agent, owned by the learner's session, labeled "Provisional draft" everywhere, with agent-suggested sources marked unverified. Attempts on drafts are stored with `review_status = provisional`. | Spec §4: unknown terms get an explicitly provisional draft, never presented as reviewed. |
| 6 | **Domain word list** ships in `server/src/resolve/domain-terms.mjs`. | General dictionaries miss terms like cholecystokinin. A fuller list (e.g. NLM MeSH) can be loaded at build time. |
| 7 | **Stack:** React + Vite + Tailwind 3.4, Express, `node:sqlite`, JavaScript (not TypeScript). | Product owner's stack preference; spec allows reusing the existing stack. No native build steps (Windows-friendly). |
| 8 | **Schedule interpretation:** review gaps are minimums from the latest qualifying attempt, not offsets from introduction; errors retry after 30 min at the same stage. | Spec §8 proposed defaults, recorded as policy `pilot-1`. |
| 9 | **Qloo through the event harness** (`qloo mcp`), not direct REST. | The hackathon supports the harness; same endpoints underneath. |
| 10 | **Hosted demo runs only this repo's code.** | Hackathon rule: the public repo must contain everything the demo needs. |
| 11 | **Camera first.** The app opens on the rear camera with a reticle; the lesson rises as a sheet over it. Tap "Gloo it" to read the framed word, or hold the mic and say the word (or "gloo it"). Permissions are requested in the tap that needs them, with photo and typing fallbacks when refused. | Phone testing on Oct 18: the page layout buried capture under text, the camera photo path failed, and clicking an existing word asked the learner to type it. (Product owner, Oct 18.) |
| 12 | **The server reads the framed crop** (Claude vision) instead of on-device OCR. Only the reticle region plus a margin leaves the phone, as one JPEG; nothing is stored. *Overrides decision 4's on-device OCR; keeps its minimal-capture intent.* | tesseract.js on a full-resolution phone photo was slow, memory-hungry and inaccurate on one word in a scene. |
| 13 | **Spoken capture goes to Google Cloud Speech-to-Text** (short model, lesson vocabulary as recognition hints) through the service account; the browser's own speech API is only a fallback. Hold-to-talk records raw 16 kHz PCM via Web Audio, so iOS and Android behave the same. | Browser speech recognition is missing on iOS Chrome and reports "not-allowed" on Safari without dictation enabled; it also cannot be told the vocabulary. |
| 14 | **Passcode gate** (`DEMO_PASSCODE`): camera, voice, the agent and lesson drafting need a passcode once per session on the hosted demo; the two reviewed lessons stay open. | Those paths spend model and speech quota; the passcode is shared with judges in the submission. |
| 15 | **Camera and mic are the interface.** The landing is a gate: turn on the phone camera and microphone, or wear smart glasses, to continue. There is no typing anywhere; the only text field is the passcode, and a shared link (`?code=`) removes even that. | Phone test on Oct 19: everything after capture still felt like a web form. (Product owner.) |
| 16 | **Favorite picks replace cue confirmation.** Each role offers 3-4 well-known names that all carry the same sound or meaning ("Which cola is yours?"); the pick is the cue, no "does this fit?" step. Picks are stored as learner cues (source `pick`) with the entity type, as taste signals for Qloo later. Provisional lessons draft their own picks. | Any option works by construction, the brand gets a view, and the pick is data. Qloo calls stay on hold until capture is solid. (Product owner.) |
| 17 | **Picture → sound → meaning, in that order, with the app talking.** Each pick card is a square stage of four pictures and one spoken line ("Say your favorite cola"). The learner says a favorite, listed or not; confetti; then the reveal shows their picture beside the word part, its approximate sound, and only then the meaning. Prompts and reveals are spoken through the browser's speech synthesis; the screen carries almost no text. The square stage scales to phone or desktop and matches the glasses display. | Owner's conjecture (Oct 22): an ambient image + sound bond to a brand the learner already likes anchors the meaning better than text. Pictures are the lead images of the matching Wikipedia articles, fetched once and cached on the server (`art.mjs`). |
